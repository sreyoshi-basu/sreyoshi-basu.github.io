I am interested in how linguistic structure is represented and processed: what grammars allow, and how speakers and listeners actually build and recover that structure in real time. My work moves between theoretical analysis and experimental methods, and between sound and syntax.

**Syntax.** My theoretical work focuses on word-order flexibility in Bangla. In *Scrambling in Bengali: An A-/A'-Movement Distinction* (FASAL-14), I argue that scrambling is not a single operation: whether it behaves like A- or A'-movement depends on the available landing site, supporting a position-based account of the A/A' distinction. I am now extending this to subject–object asymmetries in extraction from embedded clauses.

**Speech perception and processing.** I currently work with Prof. Chigusa Kurumada and Prof. Xin Xie . We study how listeners adapt to variation in speech, such as unfamiliar accents, and I am helping build a database of accented English speech for this research.

**Phonology and phonetics.** My M.A. thesis in Linguistics at Rochester described how Setswana resolves vowel hiatus across morpheme, syllable and word boundaries. I have also worked on schwa deletion in Jakarta Indonesian, pitch control in intoxicated speech, and archiving Dene language materials.
